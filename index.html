Texas has overtaken Northern Virginia as the largest data center build-out on earth — and since 3 August 2026 its interconnection queue has been frozen pending a statewide audit. This site tracks both: a nightly US-wide news brief, and a map of 35 Texas sites where bubble area is announced capacity, color is the camp behind it, and solid vs. dashed separates contracted IT load from master-plan or generation ceilings. Published gigawatt figures mix all three; adding them together overstates the state by roughly 2–3×.

What the map says

my read, not reported fact

Read 01Power is the product, not real estate

The binding constraint in Texas is time-to-power. ERCOT's large-load queue went from 63 GW to 226 GW in a year while the grid added roughly 23 GW of generation. Nobody can wait in that line.

So almost every gigawatt-scale project now arrives with its own power plant: Chevron's 2.67 GW of gas for Microsoft at Pecos, Pacifico's 7.65 GW permit in Pecos County with Amazon buying the site and the power, Fermi's 6 GW of gas plus four planned AP1000s, Anthropic's 710 MW behind-the-meter plant in Bastrop County. The spend lands on turbines, EPC and pipe as much as on silicon.

Read 02There are two Texases on this map

East of I-35 — DFW, Austin, San Antonio — is inference and enterprise cloud: close to users and fiber, sold by the megawatt in a colocation market (DFW: 765 MW building, 95% preleased, now ranked the world's largest data center market).

West — the Permian, the Panhandle, the Rolling Plains — is training: cheap land, cheap gas, next to generation, single campuses of 1–2 GW, and far from users. The two are valued on different logic. Ranking them on one gigawatt leaderboard is a category error.

Read 03The state hit the brakes, and that is the story

On 3 August 2026 Governor Abbott ordered an audit of every data center in the ERCOT queue — tax incentives, public money, load, on-site generation, water sourcing, cooling, community impact, ownership. ERCOT missed its 7 August Batch Zero deadline and asked the PUCT for a good-cause exemption. There is no published end date.

BNEF puts the exposure at 49.8 GW of delayed load — nearly 20% of the 253 GW US pipeline — and $8–15B of cost by Q1 2027, with the review possibly running into the 2027 legislative session. Abbott faces reelection in November 2026, so read the timing accordingly. Projects with their own generation are the least exposed; grid-dependent ones in water-short or residential-adjacent counties are the most. That gap between announced GW and energized GW just got wider.

Second, not every gigawatt on this map is the same gigawatt. Public reporting mixes at least three bases, and summing them is why most published "Texas has XX GW" figures run high.

  1. The queue is frozen. Since 3 August 2026 ERCOT has paused new large-load interconnection processing pending the state audit, with no published end date. Every "targeted 2027/2028" date on this page assumes the queue reopens; treat those dates as the operator's intent, not a schedule.
  2. Contracted IT / critical load (solid circles) — what the halls can actually draw. The only basis that compares like for like: Stargate Abilene 1.2 GW, Meta El Paso 1 GW, Galaxy's first Helios phase 133 MW, Aligned Caprock 540 MW.
  3. On-site generation capacity (dashed circles) — nameplate of the power plant, not the compute. GW Ranch's 7.65 GW is a permit for 35 gas turbines; Chevron's 2.67 GW backs a Microsoft campus of roughly 2 GW. Generation typically runs 20–40% above IT load.
  4. Master-plan ceiling (dashed circles) — the theoretical maximum for the land, and the least likely to be built. Fermi's 11 GW, Nexus Hubbard's 7.7 GW, Data City's 5 GW and Galaxy's 3.6 GW are all ceilings; first phases under construction are about 1.1 GW, 500 MW, 300 MW and 393 MW respectively.
  5. Coordinates are approximate. Most projects disclose only a county; markers sit at the county seat or the disclosed parcel centroid, so error can reach 10–30 km. Not for siting or transaction use.
  6. Two sites are outside ERCOT. Meta El Paso sits in the Western Interconnection (El Paso Electric) and parts of the Panhandle sit in SPP. They don't consume ERCOT queue positions — that's a deliberate strategy, not a footnote.
  7. Sources disagree. Vantage announced Frontier at 1.4 GW while Epoch AI tracks it at 2.0 GW; Blackridge puts the whole Abilene Stargate campus at 4.5 GW gross against 1.2 GW of critical IT load. This map uses the operator's own IT figure and flags the gap in the register.
  8. Watch list, not yet mapped. SpaceXAI is in exploratory talks on gigawatt-scale sites at Austin, Bastrop and Conroe with no parcel confirmed; Cipher's 1 GW "Colchis" site has a signed hyperscaler lease but no disclosed location; Galaxy's Caspian (~700 MW) and Selene (~900 MW) are pending ERCOT interconnection.
